Our Heritage

From a High-School Experiment to a Family Farm Original.

It all began when Randall Krogh decided to make corn-cob jelly for a high-school project. When he first told people what he had created, they laughed. But the very next day, during a school taste contest, the faculty selected his unique creation for first prize. He sold four jars that day, planting the seed for what would become a lifelong venture.

Years later, Randall learned about the University of Nebraska's Food Entrepreneur Assistance Program. He applied, refined his recipe, and in 2006 officially launched Krogh Family Farms, offering his corn-cob spread through local grocery stores and the historical website.

Today, the spread stands as a testament to Midwestern ingenuity, transforming humble local agricultural materials into a memorable culinary specialty from Atlantic, Iowa.

Craftsmanship & Process

How It's Made

A meticulous process rooted in local agricultural partnerships and traditional batch production.

01

Local Corn Cobs

Sourced directly from four local farmers who harvest corn on the ear, ensuring exceptional raw material quality.

02

Prepared With Care

After the corn is shelled, cobs are collected and stored in perforated plastic bags to keep them perfectly dry until needed.

03

Randall's Recipe

Cobs are boiled, debris is strained through a fine mesh, and pure sugar and pectin are added according to Randall's directions.

04

From Cob to Jar

Approximately 15 pounds of cobs are carefully transformed at a local processing plant into around 150 eight-ounce jars.
